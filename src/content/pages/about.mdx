## What I do

At Sigma I work on backend systems and developer tooling, mostly for telecoms and AI projects. Day to day that means C, C++, Python, Linux, Docker, and CI/CD pipelines. I care about performance, and about failures that show up in the logs instead of hiding.

Outside work I write Swift. [Glasstual](/projects/glasstual/) is a native Mac IRC client, a fork and continuation of Textual for macOS 26. [Drifting Thoughts](/projects/drifting-thoughts/) is a small iOS journal that turns a thought into a shareable card and keeps everything on the device.

## Currently

Going deeper on Python internals. Playing with LLM tooling, prompts, and agents. Writing here when something is worth writing down.

## Background

I grew up in Veszprém, Hungary, taking apart broken PCs from the '90s. The nickname comes from "Vaksi", a childhood name I reshaped into something that sounded cooler. I picked up programming in school, started university, and dropped out after a year and a half.

Most of my early jobs had nothing to do with software. A senior engineer at Continental gave me the benefit of the doubt and a referral, and I spent the next six years there. I started with unit tests on a BMW project and ended up owning features in a multi-million-line codebase, working on runtime environments and network protocols, and mentoring newer colleagues. The [longer version](/posts/continental-to-sigma/) covers the move to Sigma and what I learned about management on the way.

No degree, still. Sigma treated that as context, not a blocker.

## Cars

Six years in automotive left me with a habit. I keep an ELM327 dongle in the glovebox, and my father's Dacia Dokker has an [idle-speed oscillation](/posts/dacia-rpm-rampup-anomaly/) I still haven't fully explained. If you want to start sniffing CAN traffic yourself, I wrote a [starting point](/posts/automotive-hacking-and-know-how/) that covers the protocols, the tooling, and how not to brick an ECU.
